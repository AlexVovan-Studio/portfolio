Privacy Policy for MyChatSense

Effective date: November 28, 2026

MyChatSense (“the App”) is an Android companion app that helps users read and reply to WhatsApp conversations in another language and optionally save selected words, phrases, or sentences for language learning and practice. This Privacy Policy explains what information the App accesses, how it is processed, when it leaves the device, and what choices users have.

1. Scope

MyChatSense is an independent application and is not affiliated with, endorsed by, or sponsored by WhatsApp LLC or Meta Platforms, Inc.

The App does not require a MyChatSense account. There is no MyChatSense registration, login, password or OAuth profile. The App does not display advertising or use behavioral advertising. Google SDKs used for translation and billing may process technical information as described below.

2. Accessibility Service

MyChatSense uses Android Accessibility Service only after the user is shown a prominent disclosure and gives consent.

The Accessibility Service is used to support the App’s core functionality, including:

  • reading the visible content of a WhatsApp or WhatsApp Business chat that the user opens or selects;
  • identifying the selected chat and visible message authors;
  • synchronizing visible messages into MyChatSense;
  • reading visible message text, replies, quotes, reactions, media labels/captions, document names, or other visible message-related text;
  • reading WhatsApp-generated voice-message transcription when that transcription is visible on screen;
  • allowing the user to select and save words, phrases, or sentences from synchronized WhatsApp messages for optional language learning and practice;
  • inserting a translated reply into the appropriate WhatsApp text field; and
  • returning the user to MyChatSense after the user manually sends the message.

MyChatSense does not automatically press the WhatsApp Send button. The final send action remains under the user’s control.

2A. Optional Android Notification Access

MyChatSense can optionally use Android's Notification Listener access to show which of the user's already selected WhatsApp or WhatsApp Business chats have new messages. This feature is not needed to translate messages using Accessibility. The user explicitly enables or revokes Notification Access in Android system settings.

Scope of Android permission: Android's Notification Access permission technically permits a notification-listener service to receive notifications from other installed applications. MyChatSense's notification-listener implementation filters events to WhatsApp and WhatsApp Business. It ignores group-summary and ongoing notifications and processes only notification metadata needed to match an already selected chat, namely:

  • the source application (WhatsApp or WhatsApp Business);
  • the conversation/chat title, notification title, and notification subtitle (which can include a person's name, phone number, or chat name); and
  • whether the notification corresponds to a chat the user has added to MyChatSense.

The Notification Listener does not extract or save the message body or notification preview for translation or chat-history synchronization. A notification creates a local pending-chat indicator; it does not import message contents. The App reads visible messages later through Accessibility when the user opens that selected conversation in WhatsApp, including during a user-initiated catch-up synchronization.

MyChatSense stores the pending-chat flag and revision locally. To troubleshoot notification matching, recent on-device diagnostics may record the conversation title, notification title or subtitle, and source app from WhatsApp or WhatsApp Business notifications, including those that do not match a chat selected in MyChatSense. These fields may include contact names, phone numbers, or other text supplied in notification headers. This diagnostic information is not automatically uploaded to the MyChatSense backend. If a user exports diagnostics, they should review the file before sharing it. The optional in-app launcher badge may use an Android notification that indicates new activity in selected chats, without displaying message contents. Users may disable Notification Access in Android settings and may clear local diagnostics using the App's diagnostic controls.

3. Information Stored Locally on the Device

For chats selected by the user, MyChatSense may store information locally on the Android device so the conversation can be displayed and translated. This may include:

  • message text;
  • translations;
  • chat names;
  • sender display names or phone numbers as shown by WhatsApp;
  • timestamps;
  • reactions;
  • replies and quotes;
  • message types;
  • visible media captions, labels, document names, location text, or similar visible metadata;
  • WhatsApp-generated voice-message transcription text;
  • per-chat language and display settings; and
  • the original text of the user’s own outgoing messages.

Optional Learning and Practice features may also store information locally on the device, including:

  • words, phrases, or sentences that the user chooses to save for study and their translations;
  • references to the source message needed to manage saved study items;
  • user-created learning folders and phrase-to-folder assignments;
  • practice and mastery progress;
  • review state;
  • exam history;
  • Daily Goal state; and
  • progress associated with the App’s learning companion characters.

MyChatSense may also keep local technical diagnostics needed to troubleshoot the App. MyChatSense retains a bounded set of recent local diagnostic events. Diagnostics are not uploaded automatically. Diagnostic export is initiated manually by the user. A deliberately requested diagnostic screen snapshot may include visible text and should be reviewed before it is exported or shared.

Android backup for MyChatSense is disabled. MyChatSense does not provide a cloud archive of the user’s full WhatsApp history.

When a user translates a selected photo or PDF, the App may cache the extracted original text, translated text, and page dimensions in private local storage. The cached results do not contain the original photo or PDF bytes. Results older than seven days are removed when accessed or during cache maintenance, not necessarily immediately when the seven days elapse. Users can also save a translated PDF to a location they choose outside the App’s private storage.

Local data remains in the App’s private storage until it is removed using the App’s data-management features, Android’s clear-storage setting, or by uninstalling the App. Removing a chat from MyChatSense removes its displayed history and related caches and settings, but does not remove all retained source-memory and diagnostic records. Section 10 explains these limits and how to remove the remaining private data.

4. Information Sent for Translation

When an AI translation is requested or triggered, the relevant text is sent to the MyChatSense backend. On-device translation, described in Section 4A, uses a different processing method.

This also applies when the user selects only part of a WhatsApp message for Learning and a translation of that selected fragment is needed. In that case, the selected fragment may be sent through the same translation flow described in this section.

The App sends a request over HTTPS to the MyChatSense backend. A translation request may include:

  • the text being translated;
  • source and target language information;
  • a limited amount of recent textual conversation context needed to improve translation quality;
  • sender information needed to understand who said what;
  • the user's selected reply style (standard, business, or friendly), when applicable; and
  • limited message-related metadata needed for contextual translation.

The current translation logic may use up to eight previous textual messages as context.

Sender information may initially contain a WhatsApp display name or, for an unsaved contact, a phone number as shown by WhatsApp. Before conversation context is sent onward to the AI translation provider, the MyChatSense backend replaces participant labels with neutral labels such as S1, S2, and similar pseudonymous identifiers. Names, phone numbers, and other identifying information written within the message text itself are not automatically removed.

For translation of a selected Learning fragment, conversation context may be omitted when it is not needed for that request.

4A. On-device Translation with Google ML Kit

When the free AI translation allowance is exhausted, supported incoming text messages can be translated using Google ML Kit's on-device Translation API. Translation is performed on the device; the text and resulting translation are not sent to Google servers by the ML Kit Translation API or to the MyChatSense AI backend for that operation.

The required language models may be downloaded from Google. According to Google's ML Kit disclosures, the SDK may send technical information for diagnostics, usage measurement, maintenance and abuse prevention, including device and app information, installation identifiers, performance metrics, input and output sizes, language settings, model-download events and error codes. ML Kit also uses Firebase Remote Config and Firebase Installations for remote configuration diagnostics. See ML Kit Terms and Privacy, ML Kit Android data disclosures and the Google Privacy Policy.

5. AI Translation Provider

MyChatSense uses the OpenAI API as an AI service provider for translation.

The OpenAI API key is stored on the MyChatSense backend and is not embedded in the Android application.

Requests are configured with application-state storage disabled (store: false). OpenAI states that API inputs and outputs are not used to train its models by default unless the API customer explicitly opts in to data sharing.

OpenAI may retain API inputs and outputs in abuse-monitoring systems for a limited period, generally up to 30 days, unless a different retention arrangement applies or longer retention is required by law.

For more information, users may review OpenAI’s current business/API data privacy documentation.

6. Backend Processing and Operational Data

The MyChatSense backend is used to relay and protect translation requests. It includes technical safeguards such as rate limits, request-size limits, concurrency limits, timeouts, and usage controls.

MyChatSense does not use the backend as a cloud message-history service.

For abuse and cost protection, the backend stores short-lived per-minute and daily request counters. Per-client rate limiting uses a SHA-256 hash derived from the client network address. These counters do not contain WhatsApp message text or document images.

MyChatSense generates a random installation identifier (UUID) that is not derived from a Google account, email, IMEI, advertising ID or another hardware identifier. With Premium usage enforcement enabled, the App sends this identifier to the backend to determine the available free AI translation allowance and paid-access status. AI translation requests, including individual photo/PDF page requests, also carry a request identifier so that retries do not consume the allowance twice.

The backend uses PostgreSQL to retain installation identifiers, usage counts, request identifiers, access status and processing timestamps. To support safe retries and prevent duplicate charging of the free allowance, it also saves completed AI translation responses associated with those identifiers. These responses can contain translated message text and, for photo/PDF translations, extracted original text and translated text. This is a request-result store, not a complete cloud archive of WhatsApp conversations.

The current backend does not implement automatic expiry of completed translation-response records or purchase and entitlement records. These records may therefore remain in server storage after the App has been cleared or uninstalled. Requests concerning their deletion can be made as described in Section 10.

Technical infrastructure may also process IP addresses and ordinary network and operational metadata necessary to provide, secure, and troubleshoot the service.

If the user voluntarily uses the in-app “Report a problem” feature, the App sends the text entered by the user to the MyChatSense backend together with limited technical context: the App version name and version code, Android SDK version, device manufacturer and model, and the App language. The report does not automatically attach WhatsApp conversation history, raw audio, or a diagnostic export.

The backend forwards that report by email to the developer using the Resend email-delivery service. Feedback messages and their technical context may therefore be processed by Resend and retained in the developer’s email mailbox for as long as reasonably needed to investigate the reported problem, provide support, maintain service records, or meet applicable legal requirements. Users may contact the developer using the address in Section 13 with privacy questions or deletion requests concerning submitted feedback.

6A. Premium Access, Google Play Billing and Refunds

MyChatSense offers a limited free AI translation allowance and optional Premium access through Google Play. Subscription purchases, eligible complimentary access and purchase restoration use Google Play Billing. MyChatSense does not create a separate user account or use Google Sign-In for these features.

When a user purchases or restores access, the App sends the Google Play purchase token and relevant purchase information to the MyChatSense backend. The backend verifies the purchase with the Google Play Developer API and, where required, acknowledges it. The backend stores a SHA-256 hash of the purchase token, product and base-plan identifiers, entitlement state, relevant expiry dates and the association with the installation identifier. These records are used to grant or restore access, account for free usage and prevent misuse. The raw purchase token is processed for verification and is not stored as the entitlement identifier.

Google Play processes the payment, manages the user's payment account and subscription, and handles payment-related information under its own terms and privacy policy. MyChatSense does not receive the user's payment-card number, bank-account details or Google account password. Users can manage or cancel subscriptions through Google Play subscriptions. Cancelling a subscription or uninstalling the App does not by itself erase purchase records.

Google Play may send real-time developer notifications through Google Cloud Pub/Sub concerning purchases, renewals, cancellations, expiry, refunds or other purchase-state changes. The backend stores notification identifiers, event information and, where applicable, a purchase-token hash to prevent duplicate processing and synchronize access with Google Play. A pending refund-review notification may additionally be recorded with its Google Play order ID, pending refund token and refund reason for review. Such a notification does not itself approve a refund or revoke access.

7. Photos, Videos, Documents, Voice Messages, Dictation, and Speech Features

MyChatSense does not upload the underlying WhatsApp binary media file merely because a selected chat contains a photo, video, PDF, or voice message.

Photo and PDF translation initiated by the user is different. When a user selects a photo or PDF in MyChatSense (including through Android’s Share menu) and taps Translate, the App converts the selected image or each PDF page into JPEG image data and sends it over HTTPS to the MyChatSense backend. The backend forwards the page image to OpenAI for text recognition and translation. Up to five PDF pages can be processed separately. The original PDF file is not uploaded as a PDF; its rendered page images are uploaded. Those images may contain personal or confidential information, so users should consider the contents before requesting translation.

The MyChatSense backend does not intentionally store uploaded document images as files. Extracted source text and translated text may be retained in the local cache described in Section 3 and in the server-side request-result store described in Section 6. OpenAI’s processing and retention practices described in Section 5 apply to the transmitted images as well as text.

When synchronizing an already selected WhatsApp conversation through Accessibility, rather than translating a separately selected file, the App may use only text that is visible in the WhatsApp interface, such as:

  • a caption;
  • a document name;
  • a visible media label;
  • visible location/address text; or
  • WhatsApp-generated voice-message transcription.

For WhatsApp voice messages, MyChatSense reads the visible transcription text when available; it does not send the raw WhatsApp voice-message audio to the MyChatSense translation backend.

If the user chooses voice dictation for a reply or starts a spoken Learning or Practice exercise, speech recognition is provided through Android/system speech-recognition functionality. MyChatSense receives the recognized text for translation, practice, or answer evaluation. MyChatSense does not save the raw microphone audio as an audio file. Processing performed by the device’s speech-recognition provider is subject to that provider’s own privacy practices.

For pronunciation and spoken prompts in Learning and Practice, text may be passed to the Android text-to-speech engine selected on the device. Depending on the text-to-speech engine configured by the user or device manufacturer, speech generation may occur on the device or may involve that provider’s service and is subject to that provider’s own privacy practices.

8. Service Providers

To provide the translation service, paid access and optional problem-reporting feature, MyChatSense uses the following services:

  • Railway for backend hosting and PostgreSQL storage;
  • OpenAI API for AI-assisted translation;
  • Google Play and Google Cloud Pub/Sub for purchases, verification and purchase-state notifications;
  • Google ML Kit and its supporting services for on-device translation models and technical diagnostics; and
  • Resend for delivery of user-submitted problem reports to the developer by email.

MyChatSense sends information to these services for the purposes described in this policy. Google also processes information for its own payment and SDK operations under its applicable terms and privacy policies.

MyChatSense does not sell personal data and does not use WhatsApp conversation content for advertising.

9. Security

MyChatSense uses HTTPS for communication with its backend and keeps the OpenAI API credential on the server rather than inside the Android app.

The service also uses technical controls intended to reduce abuse and unauthorized or excessive use. No method of electronic transmission or storage can be guaranteed to be completely secure.

10. User Choices and Deletion

Users control which WhatsApp chats are added to MyChatSense. Removing a chat from MyChatSense removes its displayed local message history, translation cache, per-chat language and display settings, and synchronization state. It does not delete messages from WhatsApp.

Original texts of outgoing messages created through MyChatSense, together with information needed to match them to the conversation, remain stored locally so they can be restored if the chat is added again. Local diagnostic records may also remain until they are manually cleared or replaced by newer records.

Saved learning phrases can be removed individually in the App. The App also provides a Learning reset in its diagnostics tools. This reset removes saved learning phrases, learning folders, practice progress, exam history, Daily Goal state, and learning-companion progress while leaving WhatsApp chat data, completed chat translations, and general App settings unchanged.

To remove data stored in the App’s private storage, including cached photo/PDF translation results and the local installation identifier, clear its storage in Android settings or uninstall the App. Cached document results older than seven days are also removed when accessed or when the cache is pruned. Translated PDFs saved outside the App, diagnostic files exported to another location, or copies shared with others are outside the App’s private storage and must be removed separately.

Because MyChatSense does not require a user account, there is no MyChatSense account profile to delete. Clearing app storage or uninstalling the App does not cancel a Google Play subscription or automatically delete server-side installation, translation-request, purchase or refund-review records.

Users may contact the privacy email address in Section 13 to request access to, correction or deletion of personal data held by MyChatSense, or to exercise other applicable data-protection rights. Sufficient information may be needed to identify the relevant installation or purchase without disclosing unrelated personal information. Some records may need to be retained for legal obligations, payment disputes, refund handling, security or other lawful purposes. Google Play account and payment data must be managed through Google.

Where applicable, users also have the right to restrict or object to processing, request data portability, withdraw consent, and complain to the competent data-protection authority. Withdrawing permission for Accessibility or Notification Access stops the corresponding access; it does not automatically remove previously stored information.
